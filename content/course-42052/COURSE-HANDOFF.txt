## What the course must achieve
Students without programming prerequisites should build a useful business-oriented software product as a team, use AI tools critically, understand enough to explain the result, and make it available to other users. This combines compact AI/ML/LLM foundations with concrete software-building skill. Early exercises are not a demand to train an ML model inside every final product.

The final syllabus expects an AI API capability in the product. If credentials or access are unavailable, label mock activity honestly and surface the access blocker; do not silently claim the API requirement was met or require students to buy a plan without an approved access arrangement.

## Teaching style that must survive implementation
The user rejected exhausting, flat paragraphs. Each step should have one question/goal, a short explanation, one action, a checkpoint and a next action. Use Hebrew RTL. Put code identifiers and technical expressions in LTR islands. Keep optional detail visibly separate. No activity-minute labels are needed in student material.

Do not use a concept before explaining it. Distinguish the problem, method, programming language, runtime, and publication method. Students investigate alternatives and prerequisites WITH the chatbot; they are not sent to manually search a list of websites. When the chatbot needs data, show exactly how to attach or paste it. They then inspect outputs and explain conclusions.

Orientation slides introduce AI, the instructor and course; they do not repeat every practical exercise. Practical learning is delivered by the guided website/handout and Colab. Use the corrected instructor research field, game theory. Do not restore the disputed Shutterfly claim from an older biography.
